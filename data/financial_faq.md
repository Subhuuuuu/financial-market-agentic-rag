
# Financial Market Intelligence Knowledge Base

## 1. Financial Statement Analysis

Revenue represents income generated from a company's normal business operations.

Operating profit measures profit from operations before interest and taxes.

Net profit is the profit remaining after expenses, interest and taxes.

Revenue growth alone does not guarantee improved profitability. Operating costs and net profit should also be examined.

## 2. Liquidity Analysis

Liquidity measures a company's ability to meet short-term financial obligations.

The current ratio is calculated as current assets divided by current liabilities.

A low current ratio can indicate potential short-term liquidity pressure, but interpretation depends on the industry.

## 3. Debt and Leverage Analysis

Debt-to-equity compares a company's total debt with shareholders' equity.

Higher leverage can increase financial risk, particularly when earnings or cash flows decline.

Leverage should be compared with industry peers and historical company results.

## 4. Investment Risk

Market risk refers to losses associated with changes in market prices.

Concentration risk arises when a portfolio depends heavily on one company, sector or asset.

Diversification may reduce concentration risk, but it does not eliminate market losses.

Historical performance does not guarantee future returns.

## 5. Portfolio Analysis

Asset allocation describes how investments are distributed across asset classes.

A portfolio's risk depends on its holdings, correlations, time horizon and the investor's circumstances.

Portfolio analysis should consider diversification, concentration and exposure to market movements.
